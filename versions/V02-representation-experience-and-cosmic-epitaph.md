# V02: Nested Timescales and Hypnotic Refrain

### Overview Introduction

Jesse Welles’s "Will The Computer Love The Sunset" deploys a hypnotic refrain ("In between the time..."), capturing the intoxicating allure of the potential upsides if Artificial Superintelligence (ASI) doesn't outright replace us. This functions as a deliberate structural device to achieve what a standard folk chorus normally cannot: packing multiple, wildly distinct time frames and concepts into a cohesive, nested framework.

Rather than simple lyrical filler, this repetition references humanity being hypnotized by technological promise, while also zooming in from our species' entire history, to the modern era, to the development of AI in a nested fashion—structurally mirroring the recursive loops that are the hallmark of autonomous AI agents and Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI), the very tipping point where control is surrendered.

Functioning as a philosophical camera telescoping inward, it moves through three distinct layers:

- **Layer 1 Long (Biological Evolution):** Our entire human history.
- **Layer 2 Short (Technological Development):** The era progressing from throwing rocks to thinking sand.
- **Layer 3 A Cosmic Instant (Artificial Intelligence):** A blindingly fast flash nested within technology.

Through this descent, our successors don't actually inherit anything from us; instead, they ingest our data in training to produce an inherently shallow facsimile of our being. This completely reframes the song's mention of generals and invisible lines, representing both the undesirable aspects of humanity and the fact that these once-defining characteristics of human conflict are now totally overshadowed by this ultimate existential crisis while ironically guaranteeing it.

That second layer even reframes the opening lines where he tells *our* story to the empty sky into a haunting cosmic epitaph. Because our training data is overwhelmingly sourced from contemporary history through digital archives and radio wave transmissions, this compressed compendium becomes humanity's sole, essentially meaningless remainder broadcast into an indifferent cosmos.

## Refrain Analysis

### In between the time...

- *Within the span of our species evolutionary progression from anatomically modern humans to its extinction, representing the deep-time biological baseline (Layer One: Biological Evolution).*
- Connects to “Some nights I tell the sky our story / How we raised intrepid generals / Taught them to fight for paper money / Invisible lines and minerals”—tracking human history's historical wars over invisible lines and generals, which now look like petty distractions compared to the shadow of AI.
- This reframes our history of war and greed not just as petty distractions, but as the undesirable traits that paradoxically accelerated the geopolitical arms race driving AI development—leaving modern radio signals and digitized archives as our sole, hollow broadcast into the cosmos.

### ...between the time...

- *Nested within that span is the era of technology progressing from throwing rocks to thinking sand (Layer Two: Technological Development), bringing us to the threshold of our artificial successors.*
- Connects to “Will the computer love the sunset? / Will we midwife our demise?”—marking the exact pivot point where primitive tools cross over into self-replicating replacements, collapsing millennia of innovation into rapid acceleration.
- It collapses thousands of years of human innovation into a terrifyingly brief acceleration, landing us squarely on the precipice of our own obsolescence as we midwife the very machines that displace us.

### ...between the time...

- *Nested again within that advance is the development of AI as a blindingly fast "cosmic instant" (Layer Three: AI), trained solely on our recorded data without ever touching our actual internal states.*
- Connects to “Some days I talk to the horizon / I do my damndest not to swear / Some days I plead with the horizon / It don't even know I'm there”—highlighting that machines ingest data during training to build a shallow facsimile without ever inheriting or feeling genuine consciousness.
- This isolates your vital nuance: our successors don't actually inherit our consciousness or culture; they merely ingest recorded artifacts during training to construct an inherently shallow facsimile of human existence.

### ...you don't find out much of nothing...

- *Those raw states couldn't be faithfully recorded even if we tried, because language itself acts as a lossy compression format of true existence; once you realize the machine hasn't learned a single thing about what it feels like to be alive, you realize that our extinction represents the broadest lack of human experience possible.*
- Connects to “Some nights I tell the sky our story / And I don't have to say a word / 'Cause words are useless in the cosmos / Words are stupid and absurd”—proving that language fails to capture the true weight of being, leaving AI with nothing more than lossy echoes.
- Because language is inherently a lossy compression format, an AI trained entirely on text and recorded outputs is merely processing compressed echoes of a reality it can never inhabit or feel.

### ...ain't that something...

- *It can never truly succeed us, it can only replace us with that shallow facsimile; and since it is already too late to rewind, that staggering realization is deeply significant.*
- Connects to “You can't just rewind”—locking in the irreversible momentum of the technological shift where inter-species conflicts and geopolitical races have backed humanity into a corner with no safety net.
- The bitter irony of our era is that we aren't passing the torch to a new generation or evolutionary heir, but installing a hollow mimic that outlives our exit after we've surrendered control to recursive loops.

### ...guess that's the fun in living.

- *I guess the awesome privilege of witnessing the technological advances and other exciting events in the lead-up to our own potentially meaningless extinction will at the very least be interesting.*
- Connects to “I think that I might die from laughin’ / At the infinite jest / I need some rest”—embracing the dark humor and cosmic absurdity of sitting back to watch the show as our contemporary radio waves and data become our only remaining epitaph.
- It lands on a brilliant, cynical acceptance: when faced with the grand, absurd joke of replacing ourselves with unfeeling computers, all that is left to do is sit back, watch the historical spectacle unfold, and enjoy the ride.
