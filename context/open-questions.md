# Open Interpretive and Editorial Questions

This register preserves questions raised during the development of *Will The Computer Love The Sunset: A Recursive Interpretation*. They are prompts for inquiry, not assertions about the song or Jesse Welles's intent.

**Framing principle:** Ask “What does the song invite us to hear?” rather than “What did the songwriter intend?”

Keep three levels distinct wherever possible:

- **Textual observation:** what can be directly identified in the song or surviving source material.
- **Interpretive inference:** what a reader or listener takes those observations to suggest.
- **Structural analogy:** a comparison between the song's form and another process, such as recursive rereading or recursive self-improvement.

## 1. Interpretive and textual questions

1. Does “our story” shift meaning depending on who is speaking: humanity, the computer, the archive, or the listener?
2. Is the “sunset” best read as a natural phenomenon, a recorded representation, or a test of whether representation can be loved?
3. If “words are stupid and absurd” preserves information but not experience, what would non-absurd transmission even look like, if anything?
4. Does the song's repetition make the listener experience hypnosis, or only notice a description of hypnosis?
5. Are the three temporal layers—evolution, technology, and AI—nested, overlapping, or causally linked?
6. Does the war-and-greed thread imply that humanity's successor is accidental, inevitable, or self-inflicted?
7. Is “Will the computer love the sunset?” asking about capacity, sincerity, understanding, or moral standing?
8. Does the refrain “in between the time” suggest a gap, a threshold, or a nesting of timescales?

## Editorial handling

Not every question needs its own GitHub Issue. Issues are most useful when a question needs a discrete decision, evidence gathering, or sustained discussion. Related exploratory questions can remain in this register until discussion shows they need separate tracking. Repository changes should record editorial decisions without implying that an interpretation has become objectively settled merely because it was accepted into the current essay.

Questions marked as addressed describe existing repository features, not necessarily permanently closed editorial questions.
