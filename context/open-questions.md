# Open Interpretive, Structural, and Editorial Questions

This is the repository's master register of open questions about *Will The Computer Love The Sunset: A Recursive Interpretation*, the development of that interpretation, and the archive that preserves it. It combines the original 28 questions with 30 questions extracted from a later methodological discussion of recursion, evidence, and self-evaluation. The original questions and their existing status notes are retained; the 30 additions are numbered Q29–Q58.

These questions are prompts for inquiry, not assertions about the song or Jesse Welles's intent. They are not all equally actionable, and their inclusion does not imply that each needs a separate GitHub Issue.

**Framing principle:** Ask “What does the song invite us to hear?” rather than “What did the songwriter intend?”

Keep three levels distinct wherever possible:

- **Textual observation:** what can be directly identified in the song or surviving source material.
- **Interpretive inference:** what a reader or listener takes those observations to suggest.
- **Structural analogy:** a comparison between the song's form and another process, such as recursive rereading or recursive self-improvement.

## Editorial handling

Not every question needs its own GitHub Issue. Issues are most useful when a question needs a discrete decision, evidence gathering, or sustained discussion. Related exploratory questions can remain in this register until discussion shows they need separate tracking. Repository changes should record editorial decisions without implying that an interpretation has become objectively settled merely because it was accepted into the current essay.

Questions marked as addressed describe existing repository features, not necessarily permanently closed editorial questions. The 30 additions extend the register's attention from interpreting the song to evaluating the recursive process and the archive's evidentiary role. They do not presume that the archive's development proves the essay's thesis; they make that relationship available for examination.
