# Inference register

This directory separates the repository's interpretive concepts from its source records. Each concept is documented independently so that a reader can distinguish observation, inference, analogy, resonance, and unresolved attribution.

## Confidence scale

- **High:** directly supported by the preserved text/source record or by a clearly demonstrated structural fact. This does not mean the interpretation is objectively true; it means the evidentiary basis is strong for the narrower claim being made.
- **Moderate:** supported by multiple textual or structural observations, but dependent on interpretation.
- **Low:** plausible and useful, but substantially dependent on inference, analogy, or unresolved assumptions.
- **Unknown / disputed:** the repository does not currently have enough evidence to resolve the claim.

Confidence attaches to the **specific claim**, not to an entire interpretation or source.

## Both-sides citation rule

When an interpretive comparison depends on two works, cite or identify **both sides of the comparison**. A source establishing a feature of one work does not establish the corresponding feature of the other.

For example, an *Infinite Jest* structural resonance should identify the relevant feature of Wallace's novel and separately identify the relevant lyric or structural feature of Welles's song. The comparison itself is an inference and should not be presented as though either source directly states it.

Where the relationship is an allusion or influence claim, the evidentiary burden is higher: textual similarity alone is not enough to establish authorial intention or historical influence.

## General boundary

The register does not convert interpretation into fact. Claims about Jesse Welles's intent remain interpretive unless independently evidenced. Claims about future AI capabilities, consciousness, or succession remain open questions rather than established outcomes.
