## Concepts to distinguish
- **Recursive interpretation:** each new interpretive pass uses the earlier interpretation as part of its context.
- **Retrospective foreshadowing:** later context changes how an earlier line is understood. This describes a reader/listener effect and does not by itself prove deliberate authorial design.
- **Recursive self-improvement (RSI):** a system modifies or improves its own capabilities in ways that can affect later improvement.
- **Representation versus experience:** a record can convey information about an experience without being identical to the experience.
- **“Our story”:** a deliberately explored ambiguity in the interpretation. The pronoun may refer to living people, humanity across history, the narrator and listener, the cultural record, or a possible successor's reconstruction of that record.
- **“The Computer”:** the definite article can suggest a singular or mythic successor and supports a possible artificial superintelligence (ASI) reading. This is interpretive, not confirmed authorial intent.
- **ASI:** artificial superintelligence, used as a possible interpretive horizon, not as a prediction that a particular outcome is inevitable.
- **Machine subjectivity:** the distinction between records and experiences does not prove that AI cannot have subjective experience. That remains an open question.
